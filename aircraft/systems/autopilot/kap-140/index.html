    The KAP 140 Autopilot¶

    From the README¶

    The autopilot is one of the areas where I'm claiming artistic license. While I fixed a couple of "realism gaps" with the default KAP 140, I also created new ones. Aerosoft put the KAP 140 in the Twotter for two reasons, afaik: users are familiar with it and the mission statement for the 2020 version was to use default avionics wherever possible. The visual model has all the remnants of the Collins AP-106 (I think) simulated in the Aerosoft Twin Otter X Extended: the annunciators, the mode and altitude panels, the multifunction "knob" on the control column pedestal, the YD button. The 2020 Twotter's AP is already an unholy abomination, and I leaned into that.

    The main realism features are: - capture only when armed (this was a bitch to get right); - "Vertical Trim": tapping UP/DN nudges the aircraft in steps of 20 ft (another bitch) and - holding UP/DN initiates a climb/descend at 500 fpm that is maintained until the button is released (yeah, a bitch, as well); - obnoxiously long pre-flight test followed by - a 30s pitch lockout (a red P on display replaces the dedicated warning light which is missing from our visual model).

    The main realism gaps are: - from the Collins AP, we inherit support for IAS/FLC mode, yaw damper, flight director, - mode buttons and altitude selector are synced with the KAP 140 and - the Collins annunciators are somehow magically driven by the KAP 140.

    Known issues: underneath, we use the sim's AP with all its quirks. The AP still uses the Captain's HSI baro instead of its own.

    • Altitude alerter runs a 3-second self-test.
    • Pitch-trim arrow indicator is now a runaway-trim warning indicator (flashes for end-of-travel and same-direction-≥2 s commands).
    • Enabled autopilot annunciators for approach arm/capture when using GPS navigation.
    • Three-row LCD layout matching the physical hardware. PFT timing matches a reference video (≈40 s).
    • Preflight test plays a completion chime when it ends.
    • Daylight-aware unpowered LCD rendering: the display shows the dim ghost segments of an unlit LCD.
    • Yaw-Damper (YD) annunciator support.
    • Flashing AP-master annunciator with a 5-second flash on disconnect; cavalry-charge tone wired to the disconnect event.
    • Held UP/DN ramps the vertical reference; short presses "nudge" by 20 ft (ALT mode), 100 fpm (VS mode) or 5 kts (FLC).
    • FLC mode displays the selected airspeed; using UP/DN snaps the reference to the nearest 5 kt boundary.
    • Sticky BARO reminder flashes after the preflight test until the pilot acknowledges by pressing BARO or changing the setting.
    • Pitch-axis lockout (blinking "P" indicator) for 30 seconds after the preflight test and after every in-flight power cycle, while the accelerometer settles.
    • G-load monitor auto-disengages the autopilot if the pilot pulls or pushes through a threshold.
    • Experimental: NAV "pre-arm" allows arming NAV when the sim AP refuses it. While "pre-armed", the NAV ARM flag blinks in a slow rhythm. When it fails to capture, toggle NAV mode off and on again.
    • The flight director command bars on the attitude indicator can be hidden via the Disable Flight Director toggle in the EFB Config tab.
    • Lateral and altitude-capture logic now runs natively inside the Working Title GNS 530/430 flight directors, which fly the mod's own VOR/LOC/GS signal.
    • the NAV "pre-arm" bullet above (~262): relaxed NAV arming can now be switched off with the new "Disable relaxed NAV arming" Config-tab option.

    A two-axis rate autopilot with altitude preselect — pitch and roll, no yaw damper, no autothrottle, a 25° bank limit, and a small LCD that tells you exactly what it is doing and nothing more. This page covers the unit itself; Approaches, VNAV and the GNS covers flying it against the navigators.

    One piece of history you will notice in the cockpit: the visual model carries leftovers from the Collins AP-106 this panel once held — an IAS button, a YD button and annunciator, flight-director bars, Collins-style annunciator strips. We deliberately kept the ones the cockpit hints at working (IAS mode, the FD bars, the secondary mode panel) rather than blanking them. Where this page says a feature "is not on the real KAP 140", that is why it exists anyway.

    Operation¶

    Power-up runs a real preflight test. On avionics power the unit runs its self-test sequence — about forty seconds, timed against footage of real hardware, not the ~14 s of the stock sim unit. The AP cannot be engaged until PFT completes, and for 30 seconds afterwards the pitch axis is locked out — a blinking red P on the display. The red P is also the G-limit indicator: exceeding roughly +2.0/−0.5 g while engaged locks pitch engagement, and a serious exceedance disconnects the autopilot outright. Pulling avionics power in flight buys you a fresh PFT and a longer, randomized lockout — the real manual warns the accelerometers may need minutes to settle. Loading into a flight already in progress skips all of this, as a warm, tested unit should.

    Engage and you get ROL and VS. The AP button engages wing-leveling and holds your current vertical speed — whatever it is. The unit does not level off for you and does not know what you meant; check the VS readout immediately after engaging. Disengaging flashes the AP annunciator for five seconds. The autopilot also lets go on its own if its attitude source dies — a failed or spun-down gyro takes the AP with it.

    Lateral modes. HDG flies the heading bug — and only the bug; set it before you press the button. When you arm NAV or APR from heading mode, the HDG annunciator flashes for five seconds as a reminder that the bug no longer steers the aircraft. NAV tracks whatever the HSI shows: a VOR radial, a localizer, or — when the CDI source is GPS — the flight plan, annunciated GPS and flown with proper turn anticipation. REV is the back-course mode: front-course set on the OBS, reverse sensing handled by the unit.

    NAV arms until you are established. Press NAV well off the radial and you get NAV ARM: the unit keeps wings level (or your heading, if you keep HDG engaged toward an intercept) until the needle comes alive and the capture criteria hold — then it captures and turns on course. Intercepts beyond about 45° are not recommended, same as the real unit. A capture that never becomes possible — wrong radial, dead receiver — stays armed rather than lunging at noise; see the radio note below. With Strict NAV arm enabled in the EFB Config tab, arming additionally requires a usable signal at press time, so a press against a dead needle is rejected immediately instead of armed indefinitely.

    Vertical modes. UP/DN in VS mode: a tap is 100 fpm, holding the button ramps at about 300 fpm per second. ALT holds the altitude at engagement; taps nudge it 20 ft, holding ramps at 500 fpm. IAS — the AP-106 leftover — holds airspeed with pitch: taps 5 kt, held ramps 15 kt/s. The rotary knobs set the altitude preselect, shown on the drum above the unit.

    The altitude preselect captures automatically — unless you ask it not to. By default the sim's behavior stands: set the preselect, climb or descend at it, and the unit captures. With Realistic altitude arm enabled (EFB Config tab), the unit behaves like the real two-axis KAP 140: the preselected altitude must be armed — the ARM button toggles it, ALT ARM annunciates — or the aircraft flies straight through the target. You may arm any altitude, including one behind you; capture simply waits until the geometry makes sense. Capture clears the arm; each new target needs a new press. This is the single biggest workload change the config tab offers — with it on, every level-off is yours to arrange.

    The altitude alerter works like the book says. Approaching the preselect, the alert light comes on 1000 ft out and extinguishes at 200 ft; after capture, deviating more than 200 ft flashes it. The alert tone sounds approaching 1000 ft to go and when departing an altitude by 200 ft.

    BARO. A short press shows the altimeter setting for three seconds so you can adjust it with the knob; holding for two seconds toggles between inches and hectopascals. The preselect logic uses its own blind encoder — your altimeter mis-set is your problem, as in the real installation.

    What it will not do. There are no voice callouts (the audio bank cannot be extended), the pitch-trim (PT) annunciator stays dark outside the lamp test, and the unit engages on a simple press rather than the late-software 0.25 s hold — all recorded decisions, not oversights.

    Under the hood¶

    The autopilot is a stack: the sim provides only a servo, the WorkingTitle GNS framework provides the brain (its directors compute bank and pitch commands — we have patched them extensively, see the approaches page), and our own code provides the KAP 140 itself — every button, mode decision, arm gate, watchdog and annunciation runs in the aircraft's C++ module, driven identically whether you press the faceplate, a hardware panel, a key binding, or send the event from FSUIPC. That "identically" is load-bearing: every AP-related key event is intercepted at the SimConnect level and funneled through one state machine per axis, which is why a Bravo button and a mouse click can never disagree.

    Fidelity is anchored on the Honeywell Pilot's Guide: the PFT sequence and timings, the pitch lockout and G-limits, the ARM workflow, the alerter bands, the UP/DN tap-and-hold rates, and the HDG flash are all implemented to the book's numbers. Known divergences, stated plainly: the alert tone is a single tone rather than the book's five short tones; the PT annunciator has no honest data source in the sim (a previous approximation false-alarmed and was removed); voice messages are absent; the IAS mode, FD bars and secondary panel are deliberate AP-106 anachronisms; and the heading law is better than the real unit's — a turn-rate-anticipated capture that does not overshoot at high latitude, one of the things we fixed rather than emulated.

    A complete C++ reimplementation of the lateral axis — modes, capture gates and control laws with no JavaScript in the loop — exists behind a development-only configuration flag, off by default, as part of ongoing work to retire the framework layer entirely. With the flag at its shipping default, nothing of it is active.

    Config options¶

    Set from the EFB Config tab. Each option is documented on exactly one page; see Configuration for how profiles and the per-variant override work.

    Disable Autopilot Auto-ARM¶

    DHC6_CFG_AP_DISABLE_AUTO_ARM — default: off

    Stops the autopilot from automatically arming Altitude capture. You have to press ARM yourself.

    Disable relaxed NAV arming¶

    DHC6_CFG_AP_STRICT_NAVARM — default: off

    Removes the KAP-140's NAV pre-arm. NAV arming is handed straight to the GPS/NAV autopilot: a localizer arms and waits to capture; a VOR engages and flies the intercept immediately.

    Disable Flight Director¶

    DHC6_CFG_DISABLE_FD — default: off

    Hides the flight-director command bars on the attitude indicator.
