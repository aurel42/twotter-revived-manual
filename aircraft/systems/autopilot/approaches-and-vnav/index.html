    Approaches, VNAV and the GNS¶

    The KAP 140 is only as good as what the needles feed it. This page covers the coupled behaviors that involve the GNS 530/430 and the radios: ILS and VOR approaches, the GPS-to-ILS handoff, RNAV glidepaths, visual approaches, and coupled VNAV. The unit itself is covered in The KAP 140 Autopilot.

    Operation¶

    The autopilot flies the real receiver — plan accordingly. The coupled modes track the same simulated radio physics the HSI shows: terrain shadowing, realistic station range, co-channel interference, glideslope false lobes. An armed NAV or APR will not capture a signal that is not usably there, and a tracking mode that genuinely loses its signal coasts for a couple of seconds and then lets go — annunciated, wings leveled, your aircraft again. Crossing directly over a VOR, the needle ambiguity in the cone of confusion is bridged by holding the established course briefly rather than chasing the swing. None of this is a malfunction; fly the procedure the way the signal coverage was designed to be flown.

    A standard ILS from a GPS flight plan is a two-button affair. Load and activate the approach in the GNS, fly the plan in NAV (GPS), and press APR once cleared: the localizer arms while GPS steering keeps flying the procedure. Tune the localizer frequency when you load the approach — the pilot swaps it active, as in the real avionics, and if you have not done so by three miles from the final approach fix the GNS posts a reminder message. Approaching final, the CDI switches from GPS to VLOC by itself; the localizer captures on a live needle, the glideslope arms and captures from below, and the annunciators walk through APR → GS as the real unit does.

    LOC without GS is a NAV function. Pressing NAV on a localizer tracks it laterally with no glideslope arm — the real unit's localizer-only behavior, useful for LOC-only minima or a level segment. Pressing NAV while an APR approach is engaged deliberately downgrades to localizer-only: the glideslope arm is cancelled, annunciated accordingly.

    Back course. REV arms the back-course mode; set the front course on the OBS. The capture windows mirror NAV's, with reversed sensing handled inside the unit.

    RNAV approaches couple through APR too. With an LPV (or any approach with a glidepath) active and GPS steering engaged, APR arms the glidepath; it captures from below like an ILS glideslope. Approaches that publish only an advisory glidepath (+V) provide one as well — including without SBAS coverage, matching the real 530W rather than a later regression.

    Visual approaches. The procedures page offers a G1000-style VISUAL approach for each runway of the destination — straight-in transition, an advisory glidepath to the threshold, and an explicit obstacle-clearance confirmation before you commit: the synthetic path clears nothing but the runway. Couple it like any RNAV +V. Disable the feature in the EFB Config tab if you prefer a stock 530.

    Coupled VNAV is opt-in, per descent. The real KAP 140 has no VNAV; the real 530W's VNAV is advisory. We couple them anyway — deliberately. Set up the descent on the GNS VNAV page and press ARM VNAV there (the faceplate has no button for it): at the top of descent the autopilot captures the path, annunciated VNV, flying roughly a 3° profile to the constraint. Loading an approach pre-fills a sensible descent to the FAF altitude. The path retires when the glideslope or glidepath captures — VNAV hands over to the approach, never the other way. If you want the stock advisory-only behavior, switch coupled VNAV off in the EFB Config tab (takes effect after an avionics power cycle).

    With realistic altitude arm enabled, remember VNAV and IAS descents obey the same rule as everything else: an unarmed preselect does not stop the descent. Arming is your job; the modes will happily fly through a target you never armed.

    Under the hood¶

    The GNS directors have been substantially modified in-tree. The signal chain is ours end to end: the C++ radio model computes a per-station signal budget (terrain diffraction, service volumes, receiver sensitivity) and derives the deviations the directors consume — the autopilot and the HSI read the same degraded reality, so what you see is what it flies. Capture gating is deliberately conservative: deviation must be valid and sustained inside the window, a localizer will not activate while the CDI is still on GPS (a bug class we found the hard way — a noisy needle at range once captured a localizer 57° off-axis fourteen seconds early), and losses coast briefly on last-known data before failing loud. The intercept geometry is bounded so a shallow intercept joins with one smooth turn instead of the stock full-bank S-turn, and the same turn-rate-anticipated bank law that fixes heading capture flies the GPS steering.

    Honest limits: the nav-to-nav handoff and CDI auto-switch are the navigator's (stock WorkingTitle) logic — we gate the capture, not the switch. GPS steering flies leg geometry without the arc-blend refinement, so tight holds and procedure turns may lag the ideal path slightly. Visual approach glidepaths are synthetic and clear only the runway. Coupled VNAV and visual approaches are our additions on top of Garmin's framework — the most "unreal" features in the stack, kept because they make the aircraft nicer to fly, and both removable in Config. Where a mode's behavior diverges from the Honeywell manual, the manual won unless this page says otherwise.

    From the release notes¶

    Verbatim from docs/release-notes-*.md, grouped by the section they appeared in. The tag is the version each item first appeared in.

    VNAV and visual approaches¶

    • Coupled VNAV descent mode: The autopilot can now fly a VNAV descent (PATH) down to the altitude constraints in your flight plan. You arm it from the GNS VNAV page or by selecting a procedure with restraints. The real GNS 530W offers advisory VNAV only, so if you want the unit stock-faithful, switch this off on the EFB Config tab. (v2024.3.130)
    • Visual approaches: The GNS now offers per-runway VISUAL approaches with a 3° glidepath. Can be disabled on the Config tab. (v2024.3.130)

    Config options¶

    Set from the EFB Config tab. Each option is documented on exactly one page; see Configuration for how profiles and the per-variant override work.

    Disable visual approaches¶

    DHC6_CFG_DISABLE_GNS_VISUAL_APPR — default: off

    Hides the GNS visual-approach option (per-runway VISUAL approaches with a 3° glidepath, flown coupled). The real GNS 530W/430W has none; enable this to keep the unit stock-faithful.

    Disable coupled VNAV¶

    DHC6_CFG_DISABLE_GNS_COUPLED_VNAV — default: off

    By default the autopilot can fly a coupled VNAV descent (PATH) to flight-plan altitude constraints, armed from the GNS VNAV page. The real GNS 530W has advisory VNAV only — enable this to restore that. Takes effect after a GNS power cycle.
